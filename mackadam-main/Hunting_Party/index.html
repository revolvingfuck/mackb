Hunting Party

A Frost Dieselpunk Western

Prologue — Silver Blood

I. The Sun Men

They called themselves Sun Men. Brothers in an ancient order, knights of a secret war fought in the shadows of civilization. They carried runic katanas—blades etched with the power of every abomination they'd ever put down. Vampire Lords. Alpha Lycans. Wendigos. Nephilim.

The monsters whispered another name for them: The Spike.

Civilians who'd heard rumors called them SOBS—a bastardization of their formal title, Spike of Blood Silenced. They said it like a joke. Sons of bitches. Ghost stories for drunks.

Say it to someone who'd seen their work, though. Someone who'd watched a Sun Man clear a nest. You'd get corrected real fast.

II. Clive

Clive was one of them. Trained from youth. Etiquette and execution. The discipline lived in his bones—when to speak, when to stay silent, how to read a room for threats, how to put a creature down clean. He was good at the work. Believed in it.

He fell in love with a woman whose smile made him forget the blood on his hands.

He didn't know what she was.

When he found out, he did what Sun Men do.

It was silent. It was quick.

Her mouth shaped a word—please—but he couldn't let her finish it. If she finished it, he wouldn't be able to do what needed to be done. So the blade found her chest before the sound left her lips.

His tears fell. So did hers.

Hers turned to silver.

III. What He Didn't Know

She was pregnant.

The rune in his blade—the nephilim rune, her rune now, burned into the steel with her dying grace—activated for the first and only time in a way he never intended.

It could resurrect. Once per battle. But only for someone you love.

She loved the child inside her. Even as she died, that love poured through the blade, through the rune, into the impossible.

The baby crawled out of a pool of silver blood.

No cry. Just arrival. Eyes open. Already alive in a way that shouldn't be possible.

Clive stood there with the blade still in his hand. The training said: kill it. Finish the job. The mother was nephilim, the child is nephilim, the mission is clear.

But he watched a baby pull itself from what was left of the woman he loved. Covered in her. Looking at him.

He picked the child up instead.

IV. The Rune
ᛒ

Every Sun Man carries a runic katana. It's their signature. Their badge. Clive didn't leave his behind when he ran—it was too useful, too ingrained, too much a part of how he fights.

The nephilim rune sits in that blade now. A memento. The last piece of her, carried into every battle. It can bring back someone you love from the edge of death.

But never her.

It works on Clive automatically—not because he loves himself. He doesn't. It works because B loves him. His child's heart, big enough to cover for his own hollowed-out one, keeps triggering the rune whenever he falls.

He's been surviving on borrowed grace.

V. The Lie

B heard once that their mother was turned vampire. That Clive, romantically and mercifully, had to dispatch her before she became a monster.

B respects this story. Admires their father's strength. Understands why he's so driven.

Clive never corrected it.

The truth is a bomb waiting to go off.

VI. Father and Child

The weight they carry together

Clive — lever-action shotguns, carbines, a rune-carved katana. Exile. Killer. Father. He doesn't say much. The Sun Men trained silence into him, and guilt finished the job. What's left to say that could ever make it right? He shows his love through action—through protection, through teaching, through the small kindnesses he carves out of a brutal world.

B — handheld catapult, field traps, medicine, machines, lore. Prodigy. Tinkerer. Turret gunner. One-quarter nephilim. One-eighth angel. The blood shows in their genius—not just for herbs and mechanics, but for emotion and intuition. They read people. They read situations. They are warm and loving because their father, their whole life, has shown them by example that is the way to be.

VII. Synergy

They hunt together. Clive handles the violence—the shotgun blasts, the katana work, the bloody geometry of combat. B handles everything else—the traps laid beforehand, the medicine after, the lore that tells them what they're facing and how to kill it.

FATHER & CHILD

DOROTHY

When they ride Dorothy—their hover-sled, long and gnarly, named like a horse—Clive drives and B mans the turret. When they camp, B maintains the gear while Clive keeps watch. When the world goes sideways, they move like two parts of one machine.

The Sun Men are still hunting them. The monsters still need killing. And somewhere out there, a Wendigo guards a fabled utopian valley that might finally give them a place to rest.

They talk at campfires. Long rides. Star gazing. Hunting for food. Clive can choose to be functional—"check this," "watch how this is done"—or he can open up. "Do you remember being a little one?" "Remember the first time we went fishing?"

And B asks questions back. Innocent curiosity that cuts like silver.

"What was mom like?"

"Why did we leave home?"

"Dad... why are the Sun Men after us?"

The frozen wasteland stretches ahead.
The past follows behind.
And somewhere between the two—

Dorothy's engine hums.
