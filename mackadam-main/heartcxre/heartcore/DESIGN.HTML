HEARTCORE

A narrative action RPG exploring trauma recovery through three protagonists fighting cyborg gangs that harvest human suffering as renewable energy

Core Concept

HEARTCORE literalizes how exploitative systems feed on human suffering, transforming abstract psychological damage into a tangible resource that can be fought, reclaimed, and converted back into healing. The game explores trauma recovery through the framework of community-based healing over individual recovery.

The Central Innovation

Eli develops the capacity to reverse the cyborg energy harvesting process, converting stolen trauma back into healing energy for the party. This represents not merely defeating exploitative systems but actively reclaiming what they have stolen and repurposing it for restoration.

Main Characters

Luto, Aya, and Eli are the three protagonists driving the story below. The full playable roster is six: they're later joined by Gurtohli, Corrine, and Myc.

Luto
Paladin / Berserker
Background

A homeless youth who developed protective charm and optimism as survival mechanisms following abandonment trauma. His combat reflects unprocessed emotional volatility.

Combat Mechanics

Emotional volatility system that alternates between:

  • Berserker State: Uncontrollable rage sequences delivering massive damage
  • Paralysis State: Complete freezing caused by overwhelming remorse
  • Protective Instinct: Intervention-based defense mechanics
Character Arc

Multiple endings reflecting different approaches to unresolved trauma. His optimal resolution involves abandoning the search for parents who abandoned him and finding self-worth independent of external validation—acceptance of unresolvable loss rather than fairy-tale reunion.

Aya
White Mage / Bard
Background

A survivor with training in search and rescue, music, and athletics. Following a cyborg assault that fundamentally altered her relationship with her body and abilities, she retreats into concealment and self-limitation.

Combat Mechanics
  • First Aid Skills: Healing abilities drawn from rescue training
  • Performance Buffs: Support delivered through chants and songs
  • Emotional Management: Can stabilize Luto's volatility states
Character Arc

Recovery involves rediscovering her healing and support capabilities through caring for her companions. Learns to manage Luto's emotional volatility while he provides the warmth and persistence necessary for her re-engagement.

Eli
Black Mage / Blue Mage
Background

A nonverbal survivor of a house fire that killed his family. Communicates through pastels and sketchbook illustrations that literally come to life as spells.

Combat Mechanics
  • Emotional Painting: Creates destructive magic through artistic expression
  • Blue Magic Mimicry: Can copy and adapt enemy abilities
  • Trauma Reversal: Converts harvested trauma energy back into healing for the party
Character Arc

Develops new methods of communication throughout the journey. His connection to the Heartolith reveals deeper understanding that he struggles to convey, leading to encounters with his shadow self and eventual growth.

Gurtohli
Thief / Security
Party Role

Joins the party later as a trust-based ally, run through the same Interiority-gated command system as Aya and Eli rather than direct real-time control.

Corrine
Mechanic / Knight
Party Role

The second directly-controlled party member alongside Luto, fighting in real time rather than through the trust-based command menu.

Myc
Golem
Party Role

The "baby golem" whose birth is a party milestone in its own right. Myc's body is where the player's accumulated moral history is literally, physically visible — the same pattern that, together with the Heartilith Cavern, determines how the game ends.

Story Progression

Formation of the Party

Luto and Aya's Meeting

Luto encounters Aya during a second assault attempt and earns her trust through intervention rather than heroic posturing. Their partnership develops as they learn to support each other's recovery.

Eli's Introduction

Eli enters through a traumatic episode manifesting as environmental destruction, requiring Luto and Aya to collaboratively reach him through waves of shadow magic and materialized trauma responses. This establishes the pattern where healing requires collective action.

The Journey Begins

Third Attempt

The team makes their third attempt to reach the message source. The journey is interrupted by the first fight with the cyborg gang.

The Pier

Stuck at the pier hiding out, a second message from the unknown source finds them. They take a boat to the beach on another side of the mountain.

Cave Encounter

Making their way through the caves to the forest, they discover a new opposing force: a benevolent but pitiless mycelial entity. One that wants them to succeed but will test their mettle without mercy. Because of its primordial nature it cannot speak, and Eli has to recognize its intentions.

Aya's Trial

The Forest Dungeon

Emerging from the caves into the forest, the player is unknowingly at the beginning of Aya's incarnate Trauma boss's "Dungeon." Navigating to the source of the message, the party gets split up by accident and Aya is left alone.

Critical Choice Point

All dialogue choices and actions beforehand prove to have equipped Aya to handle this—or fail and get rescued. Only one outcome can occur, and once it happens, it becomes permanent. This is a major character defining moment.

The Heartolith

Discovery

Aya rejoins the others and they find the Heartolith—a conglomerate of ancient machines (computers from 2020) that form an omnipotent mind or AI, powered by mycelial networks. Called so because of its monolith-like presentation but benevolent actions.

Revelations

The Heartolith shows them piece by piece, through holographic images, the answers they've discovered through their efforts—the payoff for the player's work delivered through cutscenes, ending with hints of what to do next.

Eli's Shadow

Night Encounter

Eli, troubled with knowing somehow exactly what the Heartolith wants but unable to share this information, leaves the rooftop hideout one night. He meets a shadow boy that looks just like him.

Magical Play

The shadow boy and Eli engage in magical play, something like Eli's tantrum boss fight but fun. This will become his Trauma Incarnate, but not tonight. Tonight he gains a piece of a new way to communicate.

The Capture

Eli Taken

The cyborgs capture Eli.

Rescue Attempt

Luto and Aya infiltrate or frontally assault the gang HQ and are thwarted. Aya may or may not be captured as well depending on player action.

Major Crossroads

This is a major branching point for the game. Having the team split entirely or having Luto and Aya together to rescue Eli will lead to significantly different gameplay and narrative experiences.

World & Antagonists

The Cyborg Gang

A family unit led by a mother figure. The trauma energy they harvest is supposedly a power source, but it's actually keeping the matriarch alive through a secret terminal illness.

The actual power source comes from a mycelial entity that's been recycling old computers and radioactive waste from a past nuclear war.

The Mycelial Entity

Represents healing and rehabilitation on a planetary scale. After achieving sentience, it calculates that Luto, Aya, and Eli are the key to freeing it and suffering people from the gang's control and restoring the planet to harmony.

It is benevolent but pitiless, testing the heroes without mercy because it understands that true strength comes from overcoming genuine challenges.

Trauma Incarnates

Each character faces a physical manifestation of their trauma as a boss encounter. These aren't simply enemies to defeat but representations of internal struggles made external.

  • Aya's forest dungeon trial
  • Eli's shadow boy transformation
  • Luto's ultimate confrontation with abandonment

Core Themes

Systemic Exploitation Suffering literally harvested as energy, representing how systems feed on human pain
Community Healing Individual recovery occurs within community context, not in isolation
Reclamation Converting stolen trauma back into healing—taking back what was taken
Unresolvable Loss Accepting that some wounds don't have fairy-tale endings, but healing is still possible
Communication Beyond Words Eli's journey shows that connection transcends verbal language
Planetary Rehabilitation Personal healing mirrors ecological restoration—interconnected recovery

Narrative Structure

Interconnected Recovery

The narrative requires completing Aya and Eli's recovery processes before unlocking Luto's optimal resolution, reinforcing the theme that individual healing occurs within community context.

Player choices throughout the game determine not just endings, but whether characters have the tools they need to face their trauma incarnates. These choices create permanent consequences that shape the rest of the experience.

Multiple Ending Philosophy

Luto's character arc offers multiple endings that reflect different approaches to unresolved trauma:

  • The "good" ending involves abandoning his search for the parents who abandoned him
  • Finding self-worth independent of external validation
  • Representing acceptance of unresolvable loss rather than closure
  • Other endings reflect different stages of processing abandonment trauma

Mechanically, nothing chooses these outright. Every Core collected through the run is tinted Bright, Warm, Dark, or Void by how it was earned, and that accumulated pattern — read out in the Heartilith Cavern and on Myc's own body — is what the ending is read from: Return to Search, Citizen, Collapse, or Perfect.
