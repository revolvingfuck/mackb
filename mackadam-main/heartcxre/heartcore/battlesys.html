Hybrid Control System

A mechanical framework where trauma recovery becomes gameplay, and collective resistance emerges through synchronized action

The Dual-Layer Architecture

Luto & Corrine
Direct Control

Real-time action RPG control with immediate responsiveness. You move, dodge, and attack with full agency over their moment-to-moment survival — Luto as Berserker/Paladin, Corrine as Mechanic/Knight.

  • Handles combat survival directly
  • Dynamic berserker/paralysis states
  • Performance affected by Heart volatility
  • The only characters with full immediacy
Aya, Eli, Gurtohli & Myc
Trust-Based Commands (ATB)

Traditional turn-based/ATB style commands through a menu system. Their responsiveness is driven by a hidden Interiority stat — trust, emotional state, and healing progression all feed into it.

  • ABS meter fills based on multiple factors
  • Response time varies with Interiority/trust
  • Emotional clarity affects performance
  • Can hesitate, refuse, or misfire

The Innovation

Early game when trust is low, calling for Aya to heal might have a delay — she hesitates, second-guesses. As your relationship builds through choices and shared experiences, she responds faster and more confidently.

Eli's art-based spells take longer when he's overwhelmed but flow quickly when he's in a creative state. His painting-into-spells mechanic works through the command menu where you select emotions/concepts to manifest.

Gurtohli (Thief/Security) and Myc — the golem born partway through the story — run on the same Interiority-gated ATB, so a party that hasn't earned their trust can watch all four trust-based allies stall at once under pressure.

Hidden Systems: Heart & Core

Heart (Volatility)

Not a global meter. A hidden volatility value that modulates timing, reliability, and hesitation.

  • Affects command delay
  • Influences animation variance
  • Modulates input forgiveness
  • Controls ABS fill speed

You never "spend Heart." You lose composure, and the system shows it through friction.

Core (Commitment)

Not a resource bar. Represents which lines you have crossed — and which you refuse to.

  • Unlocks behavioral overrides
  • Governs party synchronization
  • Changes what system allows
  • Gates collective resistance

Core changes what the system allows, not how fast it runs.

ABS Fill Rate = Base Recovery × Healing Progression × Trust × Emotional Clarity ÷ Heart Volatility Key Insight: → Heart volatility is global → Trust is per-character → High stress + high trust = they push through → High stress + low trust = they freeze

Luto's Dynamic States

⚔️ Berserker State
Triggered When: Heart volatility spikes (panic → rage conversion)
Player chains aggressive inputs under stress
Effects: Faster attacks, reduced recovery frames
Dodge becomes unsafe / commits further
Party responsiveness drops (desynchronization)

Luto surges forward → party emotionally lags behind

🛡️ Paralysis State
Triggered By: Overexposure to threat
Failed dodges
Witnessing party harm
Effects: Input delay
Cancel windows shrink
Camera tightens
ABS meters stall or decay

You are still in control — but control feels thick.

Core as Party-Sync Mechanic

Core advances when you stand your ground together, protect rather than dominate, and choose confrontation with consent of the party.

Each Core Milestone:
Reduces hesitation penalties
Allows simultaneous action windows
Enables combo moments
Suppresses volatility cascades

Late game Core: The party stops asking "should we?" — The system stops inserting delay.

Underneath the sync gating, every Core you carry is tinted Bright, Warm, Dark, or Void by how it was earned. Nothing reads that pattern back to you as a score — it is read out at the end, deciding which of the four endings (Return to Search, Citizen, Collapse, or Perfect) the run has actually earned.

The Metaphor Made Mechanical

The mechanical shift from "survive by hiding" to "stand and fight together" literally mirrors trauma recovery — from isolated hypervigilance to collective resistance.
